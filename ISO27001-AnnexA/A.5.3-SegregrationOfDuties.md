# A.5.3 – Segregation of Duties

**Clause:** ISO/IEC 27001, A.5.3  
**Title:** Segregation of Duties

---

## One-Sentence Takeaway
> ISO/IEC 27001 A.5.3 requires organizations to separate conflicting duties to reduce the risk of fraud, error, and misuse, using compensating controls where full segregation is not possible.

---

## Purpose
The purpose of segregation of duties is to **reduce the risk of error, fraud, misuse, and abuse** by ensuring that **no single individual has excessive control over critical processes or systems**.

---

## Key Concepts
* Segregation of duties:
  * Limits the opportunity for **intentional misuse** and **unintentional errors**
  * Protects **confidentiality, integrity, and availability**
* Critical activities should be:
  * Divided among **two or more individuals**
  * Designed to include **independent checks or authorizations**
* Examples:
  * Dual control (two keys/passwords)
  * Separate roles for authorization, execution, and review

---

## Risk Context
* Lack of segregation is a known cause of:
  * Fraud
  * Undetected errors
  * Major organizational failures (e.g., Barings Bank collapse)
* Collusion is possible but **significantly less likely** than single-person abuse.

---

## Small Organizations and Compensating Controls
* Where full segregation is not feasible:
  * Apply the principle **as far as possible**
  * Use compensating controls such as:
    * Detailed logging
    * Independent review of logs
    * Tamper-resistant records
* Logs must:
  * Be protected from alteration
  * Be reviewed regularly by independent individuals

---

## Audit Expectations
Auditors will verify that:
* Segregation of duties is considered in **risk assessments**
* Critical roles (especially **security administration and audit**) are separated
* Independent verification exists between process stages
* Logs are:
  * Protected
  * Reviewed
  * Retained and audited
* Holiday and absence arrangements do not undermine segregation
* Compensating controls are in place where segregation is limited
